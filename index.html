SUBJECT: Market Research for Laerdal Medical SimMan Critical Care at Walter Reed National Military Medical Center, Bethesda, MD

1.    Purpose: This memorandum serves to document how market research was conducted to arrive at the most suitable approach to acquiring the services described below, in accordance with Defense Health Agency (DHA) Procedures, Guidance, and Information (PGI) 210.002-90.

2.    Description of Services Required: The requirement is for the acquisition of Laerdal Medical SimMan Critical Care. This is a high-fidelity, wireless, full-body critical care patient simulator designed for advanced training in critical care, emergency medicine, respiratory therapy, advanced airway management, and cardiac life support/resuscitation (ACLS/ALS). Key features include advanced physiological modeling, realistic respiratory and hemodynamic simulation, mechanical ventilation compatibility, real-time clinical feedback, and wireless operation. This equipment is required to support medical training requirements at Walter Reed National Military Medical Center (WRNMMC), Bethesda, MD. No prior acquisition history for this specific model was identified at this location.

c. Analysis: Laerdal Medical is the original equipment manufacturer of the SimMan Critical Care and is the primary source capable of providing the exact configuration with the required critical care and clinical simulation features for WRNMMC training. 14.   Conclusion and Summary: Market research confirms that the Laerdal Medical SimMan Critical Care is a commercial item manufactured by Laerdal Medical. Limited commercial sources exist for equivalent critical care patient simulators with the specific features required for military medical training at WRNMMC. The acquisition is recommended as a commercial item under FAR Part 12. Depending on the final estimated value, simplified acquisition procedures under FAR Part 13 may apply. If the exact model is required without acceptable alternatives, a sole source justification under FAR 6.302-1 may be necessary. Small business set-aside is not recommended due to the specialized nature and limited sources identified.
